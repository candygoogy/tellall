Politically, the empire suffered from chronic instability, as emperors were frequently assassinated or overthrown, leaving little room for consistent leadership. Economically, heavy taxation, inflation caused by debased currency, and a reliance on slave labor weakened trade and agriculture. The empire’s enormous size also made it difficult to govern and defend, which led Diocletian to divide it into eastern and western halves. Meanwhile, the army increasingly depended on Germanic mercenaries whose loyalty to Rome was uncertain at best. These weaknesses left the West vulnerable to invasions by groups such as the Visigoths, Vandals, and Huns. In the end, when the Germanic leader Odoacer deposed the last emperor, Romulus Augustulus, he simply confirmed a collapse that had long been underway.
