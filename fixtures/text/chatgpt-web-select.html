One major reason was the weakness of its leaders. Many emperors were corrupt or inexperienced, and frequent changes in leadership made the empire unstable. Another important factor was the economic crisis. High taxes, inflation, and the cost of maintaining a large army placed enormous pressure on Roman citizens. At the same time, the empire faced invasions from Germanic tribes, including the Visigoths, who captured Rome in 410 AD. The empire had also become too large to govern effectively, making it difficult to defend its borders. In 395 AD, the Roman Empire was divided into the Western and Eastern Roman Empires. While the Eastern Empire continued to prosper, the Western Empire gradually weakened. Finally, in 476 AD, the last Western Roman emperor, Romulus Augustulus, was overthrown. These combined problems led to the end of the Western Roman Empire.
